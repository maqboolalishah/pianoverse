Features

Every tool a beginner needs, and nothing that gets in the way.

PianoVerse combines a realistic 88-key piano with a patient, step-by-step teacher. Here's what's inside.

Start Playing Free
Step-by-step guides

"Step 5 of 42 — press E4."

Every song is broken into single, achievable steps. You always know what to press, with which finger and which hand.

  • The target key glows gold; the next two keys glow softly so you can plan ahead
  • Suggested finger number (1 = thumb … 5 = little finger) and hand for every note
  • Friendly coaching: "Big leap up — move your whole hand" or "Move 2 white keys to the right"
  • After three misses, it plays the note for you and the key bounces as a hint
Try a guided song
4 notation systems

Learn in Sargam, Western, Solfège or PC keys

PianoVerse is one of very few piano apps that speaks Indian Sargam natively, so millions of learners who sing Sa Re Ga can finally learn piano in their own musical language.

  • Sargam with komal (flat) notes underlined and tivra Ma marked, plus octave dots
  • Choose any key as Sa, the way Indian musicians do
  • All four names shown together for each step, so you naturally learn to translate between them
  • A small treble and bass staff shows every note, building real sight-reading over time
Switch notations in the app
Practice modes

Listen, learn, then perform

Different moments call for different practice. Switch modes any time without losing your place.

  • Listen: hear the whole melody while the keys light up
  • Step-by-step: the song waits for you at every note, perfect for learning
  • Play-along: keep tempo with a count-in, get Perfect / Good / Miss feedback and a score
  • Memory mode: play from memory with the guides hidden Coming soon
  • Speed from 40% to 120%, phrase-by-phrase practice, loop and metronome click
Practise a song
Falling-notes highway

See the music coming

Notes fall towards the exact keys you'll play. Their length shows how long to hold, and bar lines help you feel the beat.

  • Right-hand notes in gold, left-hand notes in blue
  • Note names and finger numbers printed right on each note
  • In Free Play, the notes you play rise like glowing trails, and chords are named for you
See it in action
